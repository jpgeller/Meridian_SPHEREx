# Epsilon Indi B (Ba + Bb) — facts sheet (A0)

Used for prompts, captions and the guide. Each line has a source. **"Check"** = not yet confirmed in the primary paper:
do not put it in a caption until it is.

## The system
- Three bodies plus a planet: the star **ε Indi A** (spectral type K5V, an orange dwarf), the giant planet **ε Indi Ab**, and
  far away the brown-dwarf pair **ε Indi Ba + Bb**. Source: SIMBAD; Matthews et al. 2024 (Nature 633, 789).
- Distance: 3.638 ± 0.001 pc ≈ **11.9 light-years** (Gaia EDR3, via Matthews et al. 2024).
- Ba/Bb are about **1,460 au** (projected) from ε Indi A. Source: secondary summary of the literature. **Check** against
  Scholz et al. 2003 (A&A 398, L29), the discovery paper.

## The brown dwarfs
- Spectral types: **Ba T1–T1.5, Bb T6**. Source: Chen et al. 2022 (AJ 163, 288), Scholz et al. 2004.
- Dynamical masses: **Ba 66.9 ± 0.4, Bb 53.3 ± 0.3 Jupiter masses**. Source: Chen et al. 2022.
- Age: **3.5 (+0.8/−1.0) billion years** (from ε Indi A's activity). Source: Chen et al. 2022.
- Size: brown dwarfs this old are all about **Jupiter's size** (degenerate gas: more mass hardly means bigger). General
  brown-dwarf physics; draw Ba and Bb about the same size.
- Separation of the pair: semi-major axis **≈ 2.6 au**. Source: secondary summary. **Check** in Chen et al. 2022 (orbit table).
- Orbital period: **≈ 11 years** in earlier work. **Check** (Cardoso et al. 2009 / Chen et al. 2022).
- Temperatures: Ba ≈ 1,300 K, Bb ≈ 900 K (T1 and T6 typical values). **Check** in King et al. 2010 (A&A 510, A99).
- Luminosity: Ba is ~3.4× brighter than Bb (log L = −4.70 vs −5.23). Source: King et al. 2010 (arXiv 0911.3143).

## What they look like (for the picture)
- In visible light T dwarfs are very dim and **deep red to magenta**: methane and pressure-broadened sodium/potassium absorb
  most visible light. Bb (T6, colder) is darker and more strongly methane-coloured than Ba.
- Weather: brown dwarfs show **banded, patchy clouds** (variability monitoring of other T dwarfs); the L/T transition (Ba is
  near it) is where clouds break up. The exact pattern on Ba or Bb is **unknown: imagined**.
- No visible surface: they are balls of gas, like Jupiter.

## The planet ε Indi Ab (optional, L1 only)
- A cold giant planet, **~6–7.5 Jupiter masses**, **~300 K**, at **~28 au** from ε Indi A (wide orbit, uncertain).
  Directly imaged by JWST/MIRI in 2023. Sources: Matthews et al. 2024; refinements 2026 (Sanghi et al., Matthews et al.).
